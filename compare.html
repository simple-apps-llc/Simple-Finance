Why Simple Finance

The one that never asks for your bank password

Every other app here routes your money through a company’s servers — most behind a subscription. Simple Finance keeps it on your own devices and in your iCloud instead. Here’s the honest case for that trade — and the places the others still beat us.

The short version

Free, not $48–$168 a year

No subscription, no trial that expires, no tier that hides the feature you wanted behind another $100.

No bank credentials, anywhere

There is no aggregator holding a login to your accounts, because there is no server of ours for it to send anything to.

Your data stays yours

On your device and, if you turn sync on, in your own private iCloud. We cannot read it. Support cannot look it up.

A real Mac app

Not the website in a window. Sidebar, keyboard shortcuts, multiple windows, and the same on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.

What you give up, honestly. Those apps pull your transactions in automatically because they hold a connection to your bank. Simple Finance does not, so transactions arrive by import — CSV, OFX, QFX, Excel, or a photo of a statement read on device — or by typing the ones that matter.

If a nightly automatic feed is the feature you are buying, one of those is the better app for you, and we would rather say so. If you would rather your bank credentials and your spending history not sit with a third party at all, that is the entire reason this one exists.

The others, fairly

These are all good apps. They are built on a different bargain, and for a lot of people that bargain is the right one.

YNAB $109 a year

A method as much as an app: zero-based budgeting where every dollar is given a job before the month starts. The teaching material is genuinely good and the following is devoted. It has an Apple Watch app and no AI features at all, which for a lot of people is the point.

It runs in a browser on the Mac rather than as a Mac app, it is a subscription, and your transactions sync through YNAB's servers. If the envelope method is what you are buying, buy it — it is the best expression of that method there is.

Monarch $99.99 a year, or $199 for Plus

The most common Mint replacement. Broad account coverage, forward-looking cash flow, dashboards built for two people to share, and an AI assistant you can ask questions in plain language.

The rental and business tools are on the $199 Plus tier, not the $99.99 one. It is a web app on the Mac, and the AI runs on Monarch's servers, which means your figures do too. If you want one place a couple can both log into from anything, it is the strongest option here.

Copilot $95 a year

The closest of the four to this app in feel, and the one we would recommend first if you want the automatic feed. Genuinely native on Mac and iPhone, carefully designed, and widely praised for how well it categorizes transactions on its own.

Still a subscription, still built on a bank connection, and your history still lives on their servers. Apple-only, like this one. If you want automatic categorization in a beautiful native app and the subscription is fine, Copilot is a good buy.

Quicken Simplifi $47.88 a year

The cheapest of the four and the most conventional. A spending-plan approach from Quicken, who have been doing this longer than anyone, with solid account coverage and a clear monthly view.

Web and mobile only — no Mac app — and the same structure as the others underneath: a subscription, an account with the vendor, and your transactions passing through their systems.

EveryDollar Free, or $79.99 a year for sync

Ramsey Solutions' zero-based budget, and the app the “baby steps” crowd reaches for. The free tier does manual budgeting well, the layout is deliberately simple, and there is a large, motivated community around the method.

Bank sync and paycheck planning are the $79.99 Premium tier — the free version is manual entry only. Web and mobile with no Mac app, an account is required, and it syncs through Ramsey's servers. No net worth, investments, business or retirement planning.

Rocket Money Free, or $7–$14 a month for Premium

Best known for finding and cancelling the subscriptions you forgot about, and it is genuinely good at it. The free tier links accounts and flags recurring charges; millions of people use it for that alone.

The actual budgeting — unlimited categories, net worth, dashboards — is Premium, its bill-negotiation service keeps 35–60% of whatever it saves you, and everything syncs through their servers. Web and mobile only, and built for cutting bills rather than deep planning.

Empower (Personal Capital) Free

The strongest free investment and net-worth tracker here, with a retirement planner that runs real Monte Carlo simulations — the one rival that matches us there. If your focus is portfolios and retirement readiness it is excellent, and it costs nothing to use.

It is free because it is the front door to a wealth-management service: link six figures of assets and expect a call. Everything runs on their servers, there is no Mac app, and it is thin on everyday budgeting, business and rental tracking.

So who is this for?

People who want the whole picture — budgets, net worth, bills, investments, a retirement plan — without handing a third party a standing connection to their bank, and without paying a subscription for the privilege.

If you would rather have transactions appear on their own overnight, and you are comfortable with what that requires, Copilot or Monarch will make you happier than we will. We would rather you knew that now than after you had moved your whole financial life across.
